[Sell It!]
1: Pitch your favourite hobby to a stranger at a party in 30 seconds. Make them want to try it.
2: Pitch a completely ordinary household object — a spoon — as a groundbreaking invention to a room of investors.
3: You have 60 seconds to pitch yourself for your dream job — but you're talking to the CEO's assistant who has no hiring authority. Make them remember you.
4: Pitch a new product to skeptical investors: a subscription box that sends personalised motivational rocks to remote workers. You have 90 seconds — and you know nothing about this industry.
5: You're mid-pitch to a major investor when they interrupt: "Your competitor already has a better product and more funding." Recover and close the deal without skipping a beat.

[Once Upon a Time...]
1: Tell a 1-minute story about your most embarrassing moment — real or invented. It must have a beginning, middle, and end.
2: Tell a story about an unlikely hero who must save the day — but the hero has one absurd fatal flaw: they are physically incapable of walking through any door without knocking on it exactly three times, no matter the urgency.
3: Tell a 2-minute story that starts with: "I never expected to find a penguin in the boardroom…"
4: Tell a story with a clear arc — but you must switch genres at least three times: open as a thriller, shift into a romance, and close as a nature documentary.
5: Tell the same story from three perspectives in 3 minutes: the hero, the villain, and an inanimate object that witnessed everything.

[Yes, And...]
1: For 90 seconds, respond to every statement your scene partner makes with "Yes, and…" — accept their reality and build on it, no matter what they offer.
2: You are a new employee on your first day. Your manager gives you increasingly bizarre instructions with complete seriousness. Accept each one and ask a sensible follow-up question.
3: Have a completely normal conversation about planning the office summer party — but every single sentence you say must include the word "protocol".
4: Perform a 2-minute scene playing TWO characters with opposing views on switching to a 4-day work week. Switch between them every 30 seconds — use a physical shift (turn, step, or gesture) to signal each change.
5: You're chairing an important board meeting to discuss the annual budget. Over 3 minutes, three escalating crises unfold: a fire alarm, a goat wandering in, and the CFO claiming they're a time traveller. Stay in character, stay professional, get through the agenda.

[The Hot Seat]
1: Answer in under 30 seconds: "What is your biggest professional strength?" — and back it up with a real (or realistic) example.
2: You're a politician. Answer: "Why should we trust you?" — without using the words "trust", "promise", or "believe".
3: Defend this position out loud, even if you personally disagree: "Remote work is fundamentally harmful to company culture and should be phased out entirely."
4: You are the CEO of a company that just suffered a major public data breach affecting millions of customers. Face an angry journalist in a live press conference. Answer three questions.
5: Answer five rapid-fire questions in 30-second bursts, back to back, with no pauses: (1) What would you do with 1 billion euros? (2) How do you handle conflict with a coworker? (3) Describe your leadership style in one sentence. (4) What is fundamentally wrong with the modern workplace? (5) What is the one thing every team leader must know?

[Walk in Their Shoes]
1: You are a tour guide showing a group of aliens around a modern office for the first time. Explain what everything is and why humans need it.
2: You are a medieval knight who has just been transported to a modern tech company. React to your first all-hands Zoom meeting in real time.
3: Deliver a motivational speech — but you are a burnt-out middle manager who has given this exact speech 200 times and is secretly done with it all.
4: You are a world-renowned expert being interviewed on live TV. Your field: competitive cloud classification and professional sky-watching. Explain your life's work and latest breakthrough to a politely confused host.
5: Play three characters simultaneously in a 3-minute budget meeting: an overly enthusiastic intern who has read too many self-help books, a deeply cynical 20-year veteran, and a clueless senior executive who thinks the meeting is about something completely different.

[Plot Twist!]
1: Tell a 1-minute bedtime story for adults. It must have a moral — and the moral must genuinely surprise us.
2: Retell a famous fairy tale set entirely in a modern corporate environment. Keep the spirit of the original, but update every element for the office world.
3: Begin a story about a relaxing vacation. At the 1-minute mark, your character discovers the hotel is actually a top-secret government research facility. Weave in the twist seamlessly and bring the story to a close.
4: Tell a 2-minute story that begins as a tragedy and ends as a comedy — using the same characters and the same chain of events throughout.
5: Tell a story in reverse — start at the dramatic ending and work backwards to the quiet beginning, keeping it emotionally coherent the whole way through.
